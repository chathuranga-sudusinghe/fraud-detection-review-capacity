## Context

The study compares five score-to-review decision policies under a fixed daily review budget K_c (the number of transactions that can be reviewed per relative day in review-capacity scenario c; K_c = 28 / 56 / 139 / 278 for c = 1 / 2 / 5 / 10 %, [DR-002](DR-002-review-capacity-definition.md)):

- **R0** (default threshold 0.5 on the raw model score), **R1** (F1-optimal threshold chosen on Validation 2) and **R2** (capacity-rate threshold τ_c chosen on Validation 2 to flag a fraction c of transactions) are **static threshold policies**. On each relative day they flag every transaction whose score reaches the threshold, however many that is.
- **R3** (daily top-K) reviews exactly the K_c highest-scoring transactions of each day.
- **R4** (calibrated cost-aware capped policy) reviews at most K_c transactions per day ([DR-007](DR-007-rq3-cost-ratio-and-break-even.md)).

A threshold policy therefore produces a daily **alert demand** that can lie above or below K_c. For example, with K = 56 and 71 threshold-generated alerts, demand exceeds the budget by 15.

This study is research on fraud detection under limited review capacity; it does not model the fraud-operations workflow of a particular organisation.

### Methodological problem

Two treatments of R0, R1 and R2 are possible: "report overflow only", or "truncate to the top-K of the flagged transactions". Descriptions such as "realised daily review count" for the daily number of threshold alerts, or "frauds missed because of overflow", would also assume a particular operational outcome.

Such phrases assume what happens to alerts beyond K, e.g. that they are never reviewed. **The dataset contains no information on that.** An organisation might carry excess alerts to the next day, add reviewers, escalate them, drop them or process them in arrival order. Any of these is a company-specific operating rule, not a property of the policy or the data.

The decision must therefore:

1. define overflow and unused capacity precisely and reproducibly;
2. keep the threshold policies distinct from the hard-capacity policy R3;
3. bound what may be said about frauds among overflow alerts;
4. avoid importing any organisation-specific downstream rule.

## Decision

**Overflow rule: report-only, with no truncation.** For R0, R1 and R2:

- they are evaluated as **uncapped threshold policies**;
- **every threshold-generated alert is counted as review demand** and is never truncated to K_c;
- overflow, unused capacity and load ratio are **quantified relative to K_c** as workload measures;
- R3 remains the explicit **hard-capacity** daily top-K policy, and R4 the capped cost-aware policy;
- **no downstream operational treatment of overflow is assumed**;
- frauds among threshold-flagged alerts in excess of K_c are **not** classified as missed frauds or false negatives.

> **Research boundary.** The research measures the gap between alert demand and review capacity. It does not prescribe how an organisation closes that gap.

### Mathematical definitions

#### Daily workload quantities (label-free)

For threshold policy j ∈ {R0, R1, R2}, relative day d and review-capacity scenario c:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{d,j} &= \left\lvert \left\lbrace i \in d : s_i \ge \tau_j \right\rbrace \right\rvert \\[4pt]
O_{d,j,c} &= \max\left(A_{d,j} - K_c,\ 0\right) \\[4pt]
U_{d,j,c} &= \max\left(K_c - A_{d,j},\ 0\right) \\[4pt]
L_{d,j,c} &= \frac{A_{d,j}}{K_c}
\end{aligned}
$$

where:

- `A_d,j` = alerts generated by policy j on day d: the **threshold-generated review demand**;
- `i ∈ d` = the transactions of relative day d;
- `s_i` = the score the policy compares with its threshold (the model score; for R0 the raw model score, the uncalibrated predicted probability);
- `τ_j` = the threshold of policy j (0.5 for R0; the V2-selected F1-optimal threshold for R1; τ_c for R2);
- `K_c` = fixed daily review budget for capacity scenario c;
- `O_d,j,c` = **overflow**: alerts in excess of the budget;
- `U_d,j,c` = **unused capacity**: budget left unfilled by the alerts;
- `L_d,j,c` = **load ratio**: demand relative to the budget (1 = demand exactly equals K_c).

On each day demand either exceeds the budget, falls short of it, or matches it. The two gaps are never both positive:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{d,j} - K_c &= O_{d,j,c} - U_{d,j,c} \\[4pt]
O_{d,j,c} \cdot U_{d,j,c} &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

where the first line says the signed gap between demand and budget splits into its overflow part and its unused-capacity part, and the second that at most one of them is non-zero.

**Which budget is used.** R2_c has its own threshold τ_c for each capacity scenario and is compared with its own K_c. R0 and R1 have one threshold each, so their single daily demand is compared descriptively with every K_c.

**Link to existing notation.** For R2 at 2 %, A_{d,R2} is the quantity n_d of [DR-003](DR-003-rq2-h3-policy-transfer-fidelity.md) (transactions with score ≥ τ₂). The load ratio equals DR-003's relative deviation plus 1:

$$
L_{d,\mathrm{R2},2\%} = \frac{n_d}{K} = 1 + \frac{n_d - K}{K}
$$

where `n_d` = number of transactions on day d with score ≥ τ₂ and `K` = 56.

#### Period summaries over the Test days (label-free, descriptive)

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{j} &= \sum_d A_{d,j} \\
O_{j,c} &= \sum_d O_{d,j,c} \\
U_{j,c} &= \sum_d U_{d,j,c}
\end{aligned}
$$

where `Σ_d` sums over the 28 Test relative days. These totals are reported together with:

- the share of days with O_{d,j,c} > 0 and with U_{d,j,c} > 0;
- the mean and maximum daily O and U;
- the existing absolute deviation |A_{d,j} − K_c| and the rate/volume decomposition of DR-003.

#### Fraud-related quantities that need no operational assumption

These use labels, so they may be computed only when labels may be used: on development data now, and on the Test period after the protocol freeze. With F_d the number of frauds on day d:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{TP}^{A}_{d,j} &= \left\lvert \left\lbrace i \in d : s_i \ge \tau_j,\ y_i = 1 \right\rbrace \right\rvert \\[4pt]
\mathrm{FN}^{\tau}_{d,j} &= F_d - \mathrm{TP}^{A}_{d,j} \\[4pt]
\text{alert-set recall}_j &= \frac{\sum_d \mathrm{TP}^{A}_{d,j}}{\sum_d F_d} \\[4pt]
\text{alert-set precision}_j &= \frac{\sum_d \mathrm{TP}^{A}_{d,j}}{\sum_d A_{d,j}}
\end{aligned}
$$

where:

- `TP^A_d,j` = **flagged frauds**: frauds among the alerts of policy j on day d;
- `y_i` = the fraud label of transaction i (1 = fraud);
- `FN^τ_d,j` = **frauds below the threshold**: genuine false negatives *of the threshold classifier*, because the policy did not flag them. No capacity assumption is involved;
- `F_d` = number of frauds on day d.

Alert-set recall and precision describe the threshold classifier over its **whole alert set**. They are always reported together with the demand A_j, because the policies generate different workloads and their recalls are not comparable at equal workload.

#### Optional, assumption-bounded quantity: flagged frauds in excess demand

Deciding *which* alerts make up the overflow requires an ordering rule, and an ordering rule is an operational assumption. The only ordering reproducible from the research design itself is **score priority**, i.e. reviewing the highest-scoring alerts first. Under that explicitly stated assumption only, the number of flagged frauds beyond the daily budget is:

$$
E_{d,j,c} =
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{TP}^{A}_{d,j} - \mathrm{TP}^{\mathrm{R3}}_{d,c} & \text{if } O_{d,j,c} > 0 \\[4pt]
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

where:

- `E_d,j,c` = **flagged frauds in excess demand** under score-priority ordering;
- `TP^R3_d,c` = frauds reviewed by R3 on day d at capacity c.

E is descriptive only. It is always labelled "conditional on score-priority ordering" and is never called "missed frauds" or "false negatives". It needs no new policy, because it follows from quantities already reported for the policy itself and for R3.


### Workload demand versus hard-capacity enforcement

The decision keeps two different concepts separate:

```text
R0 / R1 / R2
    ↓
threshold-generated workload demand A_d,j
    ↓
may be below K_c (unused capacity U) or above K_c (overflow O)

R3
    ↓
hard daily top-K enforcement
    ↓
exactly min(K_c, N_d) reviews each day

R4
    ↓
cost-aware, capped enforcement
    ↓
at most K_c reviews each day
```

where `N_d` = the number of transactions on day d. For mathematical completeness, R3's daily review count is written as:

$$
\text{actual reviews}_{d,\mathrm{R3},c} = \min\left(K_c,\ N_d\right)
$$

where `actual reviews_d,R3,c` = transactions actually reviewed by R3 on day d at capacity c. R3 reviews K_c transactions when the day has at least K_c, and every transaction otherwise.

**Existing structural evidence [D].** The feasibility audit's label-free volume inspection covered all 182 relative days, including the Test period, before the split was fixed. It recorded 2,048 to 6,852 transactions per relative day (`notebooks/01_dataset_feasibility_audit.ipynb` §3, "Min / max transactions per relative day"; logged as a counts-only inspection). Since 2,048 exceeds the largest budget K_c = 278, min(K_c, N_d) = K_c on every relative day. No new Test-period data were inspected for this record; the record relies only on previously logged, label-free structural evidence from the feasibility audit.

Consequently:

- **R3** has O = 0 by construction, and unused capacity max(K_c − N_d, 0). Given the audit's minimum daily volume of 2,048, that unused capacity is 0 on every relative day.
- **R4** has O = 0 by construction. Its unused capacity is exactly the unused reviews u(ρ) of [DR-007](DR-007-rq3-cost-ratio-and-break-even.md), where ρ is the assumed miss-to-review cost ratio: U_{d,R4,c} = K_c − |R4_d(ρ)|.
- The **same** overflow and unused-capacity definitions therefore apply consistently to all five policies. Only the threshold policies can have overflow.

In results tables, **demand** (R0–R2: A, O, U, L) and **enforced reviews** (R3, R4: reviews performed, utilisation) are reported in separate columns and are never merged.


### Treatment of fraud labels and overflow

Three things are kept apart:

1. **Observed labels.** `isFraud` labels are **retrospective research ground truth**, derived from chargebacks and linked-transaction labelling. They show which transactions were fraudulent. They are **not** review outcomes.
2. **Operational review outcomes.** Whether an analyst would review an alert, when, and with what decision is **not** in the dataset. The perfect-verification assumption of [DR-007](DR-007-rq3-cost-ratio-and-break-even.md) (assumption A1) applies only to transactions that a policy actually reviews within its budget.
3. **Operational handling of overflow.** The labels do **not** tell us whether an overflow alert would later be reviewed, dropped, escalated, reviewed by additional staff or deferred to another day.

Therefore:

- overflow alerts are **not** automatically "missed frauds";
- overflow alerts are **not** automatically false negatives: they were flagged by the policy;
- frauds **below the threshold** (FN^τ) may be described as false negatives *of the threshold classifier*;
- the recommended term for frauds among excess alerts is **"flagged frauds in excess demand"**. Any count of them must state its ordering assumption (score priority) and remains descriptive.


### Research-scope boundary

**In scope:**

- measuring threshold-generated alert demand A;
- quantifying daily overflow O, unused capacity U and load ratio L;
- reporting days above and below K_c;
- the rate/volume decomposition (DR-003);
- label-based alert-set recall and precision, and the bounded quantity E, reported descriptively;
- supporting the workload-fidelity analysis of research question 2 (RQ2).

**Out of scope (not assumed):**

- carrying overflow alerts to the next day;
- adding staff or changing review capacity dynamically;
- escalation procedures;
- assuming overflow alerts are dropped;
- assuming overflow alerts are reviewed later;
- arrival-order (first-come-first-served) processing within a day. This would be a different, streaming policy; R3 is already an idealised end-of-day batch policy. It would need its own GitHub Issue;
- any other organisation-specific fraud-operations rule.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why not chosen |
|---|---|
| **Truncate R0/R1/R2 to the top K_c of their alerts** | Reproduces R3 exactly on every overflow day and equals the uncapped policy on all other days (algebra in [Why truncation to top-K is rejected](#why-truncation-to-top-k-is-rejected)). Removes the workload demand that RQ2 studies and makes the threshold policies redundant. |
| **Truncate by arrival order** (first K_c alerts of the day by `TransactionDT`) | Imports an operational rule (first-come-first-served) and defines a different, streaming policy. Out of scope; would need its own issue. |
| **Count overflow frauds as missed or as false negatives** | Assumes excess alerts are never reviewed, which the design does not state and the labels cannot show. |
| **Assign a cost to overflow in RQ3** | Needs an assumption about what happens to excess alerts. RQ3 compares only capped policies. |
| **Carry excess alerts to the next day** | An organisation-specific backlog rule. Would also make daily workloads depend on earlier days, mixing policy transfer with queue dynamics. |
| **Leave the rule undeclared until after the Test run** | Would allow the treatment to be chosen after seeing results. The rule is fixed before any Test access. |

## Rationale

- **Fidelity to RQ2.** RQ2 asks how well a transferred threshold preserves the intended alert rate and how the resulting workload compares with the fixed budget. That is a question about demand, so demand must be measured, not censored at K_c.
- **Distinct policies stay distinct.** R0–R2 describe what a threshold asks of the review team; R3 and R4 describe what a team with fixed capacity actually reviews. Truncation would erase that difference.
- **No unsupported assumptions.** The labels are research ground truth, not review outcomes. Any statement about frauds in overflow beyond counting them would need an operational rule that the research does not have and does not need.
- **Consistency.** One set of definitions (A, O, U, L) covers every policy, matching DR-003's existing sums and DR-007's unused reviews.

### Why truncation to top-K is rejected

Truncating a threshold policy to "the top K_c of the flagged transactions" would **reproduce R3 exactly on every overflow day**.

A threshold flags an *upper set* of the day's score ranking: if a transaction is flagged, every higher-scoring transaction of that day is flagged too. The flagged set is therefore the top A_{d,j} transactions of the day, and its top K_c transactions are simply the top K_c of the whole day:

$$
\operatorname{trunc}_{K_c}\!\left(T_{d,j}\right)
= \text{top } \min\!\left(A_{d,j},\ K_c\right) \text{ of day } d
=
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{R3}_{d,c} & \text{if } A_{d,j} \ge K_c \\[4pt]
T_{d,j} & \text{if } A_{d,j} < K_c
\end{cases}
$$

where:

- `T_d,j` = the set of transactions flagged by policy j on day d (|T_d,j| = A_d,j);
- `trunc_K(T)` = the K highest-scoring members of T;
- `R3_d,c` = the set R3 reviews on day d at capacity c.

Equivalently, trunc(T_{d,j}) = R3_{d,c} ∩ T_{d,j}: **R3 capped by a threshold**. That is the same structure as R4, with a non-cost threshold. This holds up to ties at τ_j and at rank K_c, which follow the existing tie rules. The rule assumes the threshold and R3 rank on the same score; a strictly monotone recalibration does not change the ranking.

Truncation would therefore:

- make the object of research question 2 (RQ2), how much workload a transferred threshold generates, disappear on exactly the days where it matters;
- turn R0, R1 and R2 into weaker duplicates of R3 (on overflow days) or of the uncapped policy (on other days), adding no information;
- repeat the collapse that [DR-003](DR-003-rq2-h3-policy-transfer-fidelity.md) already rejected ("that is a different policy, close to R3").

## Consequences

- The Test evaluation saves, for each threshold policy, configuration and Test day: A_{d,j}; and, once labels may be used, TP^A_{d,j}. O, U, L and E are derived from these, K_c and the R3 results.
- Result tables keep separate columns for demand (R0–R2) and enforced reviews (R3, R4).
- Any future study of downstream overflow handling (backlog, escalation, arrival-order processing) requires a new GitHub Issue and Decision Record.

## Threats and limitations

- **Workload, not outcome.** For R0–R2 the study reports how much demand a threshold creates, not what an organisation would achieve with it. This is a deliberate boundary, not a gap.
- **Unequal workloads.** Alert-set recall of different threshold policies is measured at different workloads, so it must always be read together with A.
- **E depends on an assumption.** Score-priority ordering is the design's only reproducible ordering, but real queues may differ, so E is descriptive and conditional.
- **Score scale of R0.** R0's demand depends on the raw score scale, which changes with class weighting. R0 remains descriptive only.
- **Ties** at τ_j and at rank K_c can make "truncation equals R3" inexact by a few transactions. The existing tie rules apply.
- **Only 28 Test days**, so daily overflow statistics are descriptive with limited precision.
